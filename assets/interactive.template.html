Same samples, different motion

A dot goes round a unit circle. A camera records where it is at fixed moments. Two candidate motions are fixed throughout: A makes 1 turn per second (filled circles) and B makes 11 turns per second (open squares). The controls change only the camera.

Before you reveal anything: at 10 photographs per second, could the camera tell A from B? What one extra photograph would settle it?

The mathematics, when you want it

Position at time t for f turns per second is (cos 2πft, sin 2πft). At t = n/10 the two angles differ by 2πn, a whole number of turns, so every photograph agrees. At t = 0.05 s the angles differ by π, so the dots are opposite and 2 radii apart. This distinguishes these two candidates; it does not identify an arbitrary motion.
